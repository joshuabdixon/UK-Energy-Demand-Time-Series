# UK Energy Demand EDA and Forecasting Insights

This repository contains code, data, and visualisations from a group project conducted as part of the MSc in Machine Learning and Data Science at **Imperial College London**. The project explores the dynamics of UK national energy demand through exploratory data analysis (EDA), with a focus on environmental drivers such as solar generation and temperature. The broader aim is to inform forecasting models that support the transition to net zero emissions by 2050.

## 📊 Project Overview

The project involved:
- Cleaning and preprocessing time series data on UK energy demand and weather variables
- Univariate and multivariate analysis of demand patterns
- Granger causality and cross-correlation studies
- Seasonal decomposition and trend analysis

Key variables analysed include:
- National Energy Demand
- Minimum Daily Temperature
- Solar Generation

## 🔍 Responsibilities

**Team Lead**  
I served as the **team leader**, providing overall direction for the project, coordinating tasks, and ensuring the group met its milestones.

**Multivariate Analysis Lead**  
I was also primarily responsible for the **multivariate analysis** and the key insights presented in the final report. This included:
- Investigating causal relationships between solar generation, temperature, and national demand
- Conducting Granger causality and cross-correlation function (CCF) analyses
- Designing and interpreting seasonally segmented scatterplots and correlation matrices
- Drawing conclusions based on statistical evidence and contextual understanding

## 📁 Contents

- `Group3_Assessment2.Rmd`: Main R Markdown report with full analysis and commentary
- `UK-Energy_Demand_EDA_Visualisations.pdf`: Final presentation/report with annotated visualisations
- `energy_demand_UK.csv`: Cleaned dataset used throughout the analysis

## 🛠️ Tools and Libraries

- R and Tidyverse
- `forecast`, `tseries`, `lmtest`, `ggplot2`
- Time series modelling and statistical inference techniques

## 📈 Key Insights

- UK energy demand shows strong seasonal behaviour, peaking in winter and dipping in summer.
- Solar generation and temperature are inversely correlated with energy demand, particularly in colder months.
- Granger causality tests and CCF plots support the predictive relationship between solar energy and demand, offering potential for improved forecasting models.

## 🔮 Future Work

- Implementation of SARIMA and machine learning models for energy demand forecasting
- Incorporating additional weather variables such as wind and rainfall
- Developing daily or sub-daily demand prediction pipelines

---

This project was completed as part of the **EDAV (Exploratory Data Analysis and Visualisation)** module for the **MSc Machine Learning and Data Science** programme at **Imperial College London**, Spring 2025.
